Step-Level Feedback Distillation

Distilling step-level score + critique behavior from a privileged teacher into a small, ground-truth-free student — so it catches reasoning mistakes step-by-step without seeing the answer.

Phase 0 complete Gemma teacher locked Qwen-27B replication pending edward-lcl/feedback-distillation
Project overview

What we're building

A small GT-free student that reads a math solution and flags which step first goes wrong — not just whether the final answer is right. Trained from a privileged teacher that sees the reference solution while labeling.

Why it was revived

The earlier version stalled (CLEAR + TinyLlama; weak scorers, gibberish on math). Revived June 2026 with a shift to step-level reasoning eval over final-answer accuracy. Target venue: COLM / AAAI / workshop — quality over date.

Where we are

Phase 0 complete. Teacher, dataset, and privilege form all chosen by data. Core claim validated at N=150. Cross-teacher replication (Qwen-27B) needs restart after an oMLX crash. Next: fix student trainer → GPU handoff.

Research question

Does distilling a teacher's step-level score + natural-language critique — and its privileged (answer-aware) judgment — into a small answer-blind student make it better at catching reasoning-step errors? Finding: privilege helps, but only when the problem is hard and the privilege is rich (a full worked solution — not a bare answer).

Why this matters

Gemma and Qwen are different families trained on different data. If the privilege×difficulty pattern holds for Qwen-27B, it's not a Gemma artifact — it's a property of step-level feedback under difficulty.

Bakeoff F1 (0.873) shows Qwen-27B is a capable teacher — the privilege signal should be measurable. This unblocks Saksham and Henry's replication section once confirmed.
